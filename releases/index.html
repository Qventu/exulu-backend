Connected tools, teams & personalization

2026-06-10

Agents that act in your business systems on each employee's own authority, image production with a human in the loop, per-user personalization, and an organizational layer for sharing and cost attribution.

OAuth 2.0 for tools

Connect agents to your CRM, support desk, or document store — each employee acts under their own account.

Tools built on the IMP SDK can now declare an oauth block — endpoints, client credentials, scopes — and the platform handles the entire authorization flow. When an employee uses a tool without a connected account, the agent replies with a connect link; after a one-time consent, the tool runs with that user's token.

For your organization this means agents act in HubSpot, Google Workspace, or Slack as the actual employee — with their permissions, visible in the vendor's audit logs under their identity, never through a shared service account. Tokens are stored encrypted per user and tool, refreshed automatically, and PKCE is enabled by default.

Why it matters: integrations that used to need custom auth code and a security review now ship as configuration — faster delivery of connected agents, and per-user accountability your compliance team will actually approve.
From the SDK
new ImpTool({
  id: "hubspot_crm",
  name: "HubSpot CRM",
  type: "function",
  oauth: {
    authorizationUrl: "https://app.hubspot.com/oauth/authorize",
    tokenUrl: "https://api.hubapi.com/oauth/v1/token",
    clientId: process.env.HUBSPOT_CLIENT_ID,
    clientSecret: process.env.HUBSPOT_CLIENT_SECRET,
    scopes: ["crm.objects.contacts.read"],
  },
  execute: async (inputs) => {
    const token = inputs.oauth.accessToken; // always valid here
  },
});

In-chat image generation

Marketing-grade visuals produced inside the conversation — with a person making the final call.

When an agent needs an image, it opens a widget in the chat instead of guessing at a final render. Your team drives from there: adjust the prompt, pick the model, set size, quality and count, attach reference images, and apply saved brand styles that keep output consistent across the company.

Selection is explicit — generate variations freely, then send only the chosen images back to the conversation. The agent never sees the rejects, which keeps context clean and downstream usage predictable.

Why it matters: content teams produce on-brand visuals in the flow of work instead of switching tools, while explicit human selection keeps quality control — and spend — in your hands.

Personal system prompt

Every employee tells the platform about their role once — every agent honors it.

A new Settings page lets each user store a free-form personal prompt: role, working language, preferred answer length, domain context. It's injected into every chat they start, layered on top of whatever agent they're talking to.

Shared agents stay standardized for everyone; the personalization lives with the person, not the agent. No more re-explaining "I'm in the finance team, keep it short, answer in German" at the start of every session.

Why it matters: better first-answer quality across the whole workforce without forking agents per department — less re-prompting, faster adoption, lower token spend.

Teams

Mirror your org chart: group users into teams and share agents, projects and data team-wide.

Teams are a new organizational layer next to roles. Assign each user to a team — Engineering, Marketing, HR — straight from the users table, and manage teams from a dedicated admin page. Roles keep governing what people can do; teams capture who works together.

Every resource gains a Shared with Teams visibility mode: share an agent, a knowledge base, or a project with whole departments in one step, with read or write granularity — the same mechanics your admins already know from role-based sharing.

Why it matters: onboarding a department becomes one share action instead of per-user grants, and the team field on every user is the foundation for per-department cost attribution.

Transcripts that know who's talking

2026-05-29

Meetings, interviews and calls become searchable company knowledge — diarized with speaker names, embedded into your knowledge bases, and processed entirely on infrastructure you control.

Speaker-aware transcripts, reviewed in seconds

Upload audio, get a diarized transcript, rename SPEAKER_00 → Daniel before you save.

The transcriptions workspace pairs an audio player with a colored who-spoke-when timeline. Hover any segment for the text and timestamp, click to jump, and rename speakers with one click — colors stay stable so review is fast even on long recordings.

Saved transcripts can be reopened and corrected later; the underlying knowledge item updates and re-embeds in place, so citations in past conversations never break.

Why it matters: the hours your teams spend in meetings become accurate, attributable records — who committed to what, in their own words — at a fraction of the cost of manual minutes.

Self-hosted Whisper — audio never leaves your environment

The transcription pipeline ships in the platform but runs on your own GPU host.

Whisper + pyannote run as a separate process on a machine you control — start it with one command, point the main app at it with one environment variable. No IMP-managed endpoint, no third-party transcription API, no traffic leaving your VPC.

Why it matters: recorded conversations are among the most sensitive data a company holds. Full data sovereignty makes voice workflows viable for legal, HR and healthcare contexts where SaaS transcription is a non-starter.
From the CLI
# On the GPU host
npx @imp/backend imp-start-whisper

# On the main app
TRANSCRIPTION_SERVER=http://gpu-host:9876

Transcripts land in your knowledge base

Saved transcripts are embedded, project-scoped, and reachable from every chat.

A finished transcript isn't a dead file — it becomes a knowledge item like any other: embedded for retrieval, scoped to the right project, and citable by agents in chat from the moment it's saved.

Why it matters: "what did we decide in the March vendor call?" becomes a question any employee can ask an agent — institutional memory stops depending on whoever took notes.

Spring platform release

2026-05-29

Five shipped: a workspace inside every chat, smart follow-ups, a configurable model gateway, single-zip skill distribution, and GDPR endpoints for super-admins.

Session files panel

Every chat is a workspace — every file the agent touches, visible and previewable.

A sidebar in every chat lists everything the session has produced or received — agent-written files, your uploads, bash artifacts — with inline preview for text, code, images, PDFs and Office documents. Uploads sync into the agent's live sandbox, so its next turn can use them immediately.

Scope is explicit: a persistent notice confirms session files never leak into other sessions, projects, or knowledge bases.

Why it matters: what your agents read and produced is no longer a black box — every artifact is inspectable, which is exactly what risk and audit teams ask for first.

Follow-up suggestions

Three suggested next prompts after every answer — opt-in per agent.

Enable suggestions_enabled on an agent and the chat renders up to three short follow-up prompts after each reply. Clicking one fills the input — the user always presses send themselves. Suggestions count against the same rate limits and statistics as normal usage, and fail silently if anything goes wrong.

Why it matters: the biggest adoption blocker is employees not knowing what to ask next. Suggested follow-ups turn hesitant first-time users into productive ones without any training effort.

LiteLLM model gateway

One config file for every model provider — budgets, rate limits and routing included.

Set IMP_USE_LITELLM=true and the platform boots LiteLLM as a supervised sidecar. Your whole model catalog — Anthropic, OpenAI, Vertex, on-prem — lives in one config.yaml, and every agent and chat picker sources from it. Rate limits, token budgets and cost tracking move into the gateway.

Why it matters: central model governance — swap providers, enforce spend budgets and avoid vendor lock-in from a single file your platform team owns, with no per-agent reconfiguration.
Enable
IMP_USE_LITELLM=true
LITELLM_MASTER_KEY=sk-…
LITELLM_CONFIG_PATH=ee/python/.litellm/config.yaml  # default

Skill bundle upload

Drop a .zip, get a skill — internal know-how distributed in one upload.

The New Skill dialog accepts a zip bundle or a single SKILL.md. Archives are validated before anything is written — path traversal, oversized bundles and malformed structures are rejected with clear errors — and you land straight in the skill editor with every file wired up.

Why it matters: the playbooks your best people write — report formats, analysis procedures, tone guides — become packaged capabilities any team can install, version and reuse.

GDPR export & delete

Article 15 and Article 17, answered in two HTTP calls.

Super-admins get two routes: a data export that streams a ZIP of a user's complete footprint (account, sessions with messages, feedback, tracking — with auth material stripped), and a hard delete that erases the same footprint in a single transaction, then sweeps the user's S3 prefix. Self-deletion by a super-admin is blocked; deletion is final.

Why it matters: data-subject requests drop from a multi-team engineering exercise to a single admin action — with an export that names DSGVO Art. 15 explicitly for your records.
REST
# Export — DSGVO Art. 15
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
     https://api.imp.com/users/42/data-export -o user-42-export.zip

# Delete — DSGVO Art. 17
curl -X DELETE -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
     https://api.imp.com/users/42

Voice input

2026-05-24

Speak instead of type — speech-to-text next to the send button in every chat.

Speech-to-text in chat

Record, review, send — dictation built into every conversation.

A mic button next to the send button records up to ~25 minutes of audio, transcribes it through your own model gateway, and appends the text to the input. Nothing autosends — the user reviews the wording and presses send.

Whisper auto-detects the language, which matters for multilingual workforces; the button simply hides itself on deployments where transcription isn't enabled.

Why it matters: faster input for field staff and people on the move, a real accessibility win — and because it runs through your own gateway, voice data follows the same governance as everything else.
From the API
# POST a recording, get text back
curl -X POST "$IMP_BACKEND/transcribe" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  -F "file=@recording.webm" -F "language=en"
# → { "text": "What's the status of the Q4 launch?" }
